2. Primary Classical Vastu Treatises Cited

Authentic editions, scholarly translators, and exact chapters underpinning our 10 Classical Vastu Tools.

South Indian Canon

Mayamata (मयमतम्)

Author: Sage Maya (Mayasura) | Epoch: c. 9th–12th Cent. CE
Translation: Bruno Dagens (IGNCA / Motilal Banarsidass, 1994, 2 vols.)

The master architectural treatise of the Dravidian Agamic tradition. Prescribes precise cardinal orientation using the gnomon (*Śaṅku*), the 81-paduka *Paramashayika Mandala*, Ayadi calculations, and the internal distribution of residential rooms.

Cited: Ch. 6 (Orientation), Ch. 8 (Mandalas), Ch. 25 (Room Zoning), Ch. 26 (Doors)
Architectural Standard

Mānasāra (मानसार)

Author: Sage Mānasāra | Epoch: c. 5th–7th Cent. CE
Translation: Dr. Prasanna Kumar Acharya (Oxford University Press, 1934)

Comprehensive encyclopedia of Indian architecture defining standardized measurement units (Angula, Hasta, Danda), proportioning systems (*Talamana*), residential portals, and site evaluation (*Bhūmi-Parīkṣā*).

Cited: Ch. 3 (Site Testing), Ch. 4 (Soil), Ch. 9 (Doors), Ch. 36 (Dwellings)
Astrological & Spatial Canon

Bṛhat Saṃhitā (बृहत्संहिता)

Author: Acharya Varāhamihira | Epoch: 6th Cent. CE
Translation: M. Ramakrishna Bhat (Motilal Banarsidass, 1981 critical edition)

Celebrated monumental encyclopedia of celestial and earthly phenomena. Chapter 53 (*Vāstuvidyā*) establishes the 32 boundary perimeter door deities and their exact fruits (*Phala*). Chapter 54 (*Dakārgala*) details subterranean water discovery through botanical indicators.

Cited: Ch. 53, Ślokas 70–88 (Dvāra Phala); Ch. 54 (Dakārgala Water Veins)
Royal Nagara Architecture

Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra

Author: Maharaja Bhoja of Dhara | Epoch: 11th Cent. CE (1000–1055)
Translation: T. Ganapati Sastri / Prof. Pushpendra Kumar (2004)

Written by the polymath King Bhoja of the Paramara dynasty. Discusses spatial aesthetics, the eight praised doors (*Aṣṭau Dvārāṇi Praśastāni*), cross-ventilation dynamics (*Vāyu-Vicāraṇa*), and environmental thermal adaptations.

Cited: Ch. 10 (Towns & Homes), Ch. 24 (Entrances), Ch. 38 (Pada Layouts)
Universal Northern Canon

Viśvakarma Prakāśa

Author: Sage Viśvakarmā | Epoch: c. 10th–14th Cent. CE
Critical Edition: Khemraj Shrikrishnadas (Varanasi / Mumbai, 1913)

Authoritative treatise detailing household domestic arrangements, kitchen hearth alignments (*Agnyāgāra*), domestic well excavation, and door obstructions (*Dvāra-Vedha*).

Cited: Ch. 2 & 7 (Door Padas), Ch. 5 (Kitchen & Water Reservoirs)
Kerala Domestic Vastu

Manuṣyālaya Candrikā

Author: Nīlakaṇṭha Moosath | Epoch: 16th Cent. CE
Translation: P. R. Shastry & Dr. N. E. Muthuswamy (Kerala Canon)

Focuses purely on residential human habitation (*Manuṣyālaya*), detailing timber joinery, staircase ascent rules (*Sopāna-Vidhi*), elevated thresholds (*Udumbara*), and perimeter courtyard geometry (*Nālukettu*).

Cited: Ch. 2 (Orientation & Yoni), Ch. 3 (Interior Allocation & Stairs)
Classical Kerala Treatise

Śilparatna (शिल्परत्न)

Author: Śrīkumāra | Epoch: 16th Cent. CE (Travancore)
Critical Edition: T. Ganapati Sastri (Trivandrum Sanskrit Series)

A key manual of southern temple and domestic planning. Sets the canonical rules for clockwise staircase turns (*Pradakṣiṇā-vartana*), odd step numbers, and the absolute inviolability of the central *Brahmasthāna*.

Cited: Part I, Ch. 16 (Stairways & Vertical Circulation)
Modern Academic Authority

Hindu Canons of Architecture

Author: Dr. D. N. Shukla | Epoch: 1960 (Gorakhpur Univ.)
Critical Edition: Vāstu-Śāstra Vol. I & II (Munshiram Manoharlal)

Dr. D. N. Shukla's seminal comparative doctoral treatise synthesizing Northern and Southern canons, establishing modern mathematical models for 16-zone energy projections and the socio-biological logic of Sanskrit building codes.

Cited: Vol. I, Part II: Site Selection, Slopes & House Planning
